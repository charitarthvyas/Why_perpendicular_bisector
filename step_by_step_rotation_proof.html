Brick by Brick: Finding the Center of Rotation

Interactive Locus & Intersection Proof for IB Grade 9

Rotation Angle: 75°

Freedom to explore: Drag the purple Center (R), or drag any vertex (A, B, C) of the original triangle at any time to test the logic.

Step 0: The Setup

We have an original object (blue) and its rotated image (orange). We know they rotated around some center point R, but pretend we don't know where R is yet.

Step 1: The Distance Rule

Focus on one point, A, and its image A'. Because A rotated in a circle around R to get to A', the distance from the center must stay the same.

Distance RA = Distance RA'

Step 2: The Locus Property (Crucial)

Draw a chord between A and A'. Then, draw its perpendicular bisector (the blue dashed line).

The Locus Theorem: ANY point on this dashed line is equidistant from A and A'.

Notice how the distances from the yellow point to A and A' are ALWAYS perfectly equal, no matter where it is on the line. Since R is also equidistant to A and A' (from Step 1), R MUST be somewhere on this line.

Step 3: The Intersection Proof

One line gives us infinite possibilities for where R could be. We need to narrow it down.

Let's repeat the process for point B and B'. Their perpendicular bisector (red dashed line) is the locus of all points equidistant from B and B'.

Step 4: The Unique Solution

The Center of Rotation (R) must be on the blue line AND the red line.

Two non-parallel lines cross at exactly one unique point. Mathematically, this intersection is the ONLY point in space equidistant from (A,A') and (B,B') simultaneously. We found the center!
